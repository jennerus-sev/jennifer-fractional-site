Association Transformation

Rebuilding the AMA around its members.

We redesigned what membership offered and how the American Marketing Association delivered it as part of a national transformation. We tested new offers, rebuilt the systems behind them, and worked with chapters to put the changes into practice.

Service design

Connect the membership offer, platforms, and support around those needs.

Change management

Help staff, volunteers, and chapters adopt new ways of working.

The challenge

Membership was declining. The organization was fragmented.

Declining membership threatened the AMA’s financial stability. Across its national organization and chapter network, more than 400 brand expressions made for uneven member experiences. Legacy systems made it hard to deliver a consistent offer.

The work called for a redesign of what members got, how they got it, and how staff and volunteers supported it.

Research insights

What the audit and early tests clarified.

Start with member needs

We tested different ways to describe membership, from needs such as finding a job or volunteering to topics such as SEO and branding.

Plan the systems together

The audit covered products, finances, technology, and staffing. It showed why changes to pricing and benefits had to be planned alongside the systems and processes that would deliver them.

Design for chapter adoption

Chapter leaders helped develop the One AMA brand system. Feedback from the phased rollout helped us refine the templates and guidance chapters would use.

Strategy and audit

Build systems that keep learning from members.

We wanted to learn from how members used training, certification, content, and chapter communities. Those insights could help the AMA recommend relevant resources and see where members needed more support.

  1. 01

    Audit the existing systems

    We audited the member experience, business model, internal workflows, technology, and financial platform. We mapped where member data originated, how it moved between systems, and what the existing architecture could support.

    The audit showed that our systems couldn’t capture and connect the data the strategy needed.

  2. 02

    Rebuild the technology

    We moved the CRM from Personify to Salesforce, replaced a customized SharePoint CMS with WordPress, and migrated legacy data to the cloud. We designed the data flows around the member experience so the interface could use what we learned from participation.

Prototyping and testing

Make the new membership model testable.

In workshops, staff and volunteers used canvases and storyboards to make the business model and member journeys concrete. We broke membership into individual benefits, then prototyped ways to group and describe them around member needs or skills.

Marketing DNA

Marketing DNA was one of several prototypes we tested with members. It let a marketer start a skills assessment, compare their results with peers, and get tailored learning suggestions.

Sequence

Make the work visible across the AMA.

The four-year roadmap put organizational, experience design, and technology changes on the same timeline. It connected the 2019 platform releases with plans for the new membership model and a competency assessment pilot through FY22.

It shows which products and features are released, what benefits they unlock, who benefits (members, volunteers, support center staff, and the wider business), and what each benefit strengthens, such as community, personalization, or agility.

Engage chapters, councils, and committees

Chapter leaders, volunteer councils, and committee members helped shape the work and test what would be practical locally. Their feedback informed the rollout, training, and support.

Help staff work across functions

We brought staff together in cross-functional squads and introduced Kanban so teams could work out loud. Shared boards made priorities, progress, and blockers visible across teams.

Implementation plan

The plan tracked technology releases, My AMA unbundling tests, data work, and change management simultaneously. We could easily update it as dependencies and priorities changed.

One AMA rollout

Create a shared story chapters could use.

National and chapter leaders co-created a brand system, then tested it in phases with templates, guides, and training. The Coach, Badge, Place booklet translated the research into three ways to talk about what members valued. When in-person events stopped, we built a shared schedule to coordinate the shift online. The brand system helped chapters communicate the new calendar and weekly digital benefits to members.

Outcomes

Stronger renewals and a rollout chapters adopted.

The first year brought financial stability and stronger renewals. The shared brand and new ways of working helped the AMA keep serving members when the pandemic disrupted in-person programs.

7.5% increase in dues revenue

Renewal rates also improved by 2.1% in the first year of the transformation.

100% chapter adoption of One AMA

By fiscal year-end, chapters had adopted the shared brand system.

3.93% membership growth during the pivot

Membership grew from March to September 2020 as the AMA shifted events and training online.

Employee engagement rose from 55% to 96%

During the transformation, the AMA formed its first innovation and data teams and organized work in cross-functional squads.
